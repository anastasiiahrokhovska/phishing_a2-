Cybersecurity: The Human Firewall

One careless click can put an entire company at risk. Can your team spot security problems, react correctly and protect important data?

A2+ · Pre-intermediate60–75 minutesIT & Workplace EnglishReading · Vocabulary · Grammar · Speaking

Lesson objectives

  • Understand common cybersecurity threats and basic security procedures.
  • Learn and use 15 useful IT security words.
  • Revise should, shouldn't, must, mustn't and Don't + infinitive.
  • Give clear advice and explain what employees must or mustn't do.
  • Investigate a security incident and create a short company security policy.

Teacher's note: This is a follow-up to “Hack or Not? Staying Safe Online”. It does not repeat the previous phishing email detective or Netflix reading. The grammar is revision from the previous lesson.

3. Reading: One mistake, one big problem

10 min

Read the story and find out what happened at a small software company.

Monday, 9:15 a.m. — The incident

BrightApps is a small software company with 25 employees. On Monday morning, several employees could not open their work files. A message appeared on their screens: “Your files are locked. Pay us to get them back.”

The IT team discovered that the company had suffered a ransomware attack. The attackers entered the system through an employee's laptop. The employee, Daniel, had downloaded a free program from an unknown website. He did not check whether the website was safe.

The program installed malicious software on his laptop. Later, the attackers accessed a shared company folder. Some confidential documents were copied, and other files were locked.

The company had a backup, but it was three weeks old. The IT team had to work for hours to recover the files. The manager stopped employees from using the company network while the team investigated the problem.

After the incident, BrightApps introduced new security rules. Employees must use strong passwords and report suspicious activity. They mustn't download unknown programs on work devices. They should install updates regularly and make sure important files are backed up.

4. Grammar revision: Rules, advice & warnings

15–20 min

Revision from the previous lesson: choose the correct structure to give advice, express rules and warn people.

StructureUseExample
should + verbAdvice; a good ideaYou should update your laptop.
shouldn't + verbAdvice against somethingYou shouldn't use public Wi-Fi for banking.
must + verbStrong rule or obligationEmployees must protect company data.
mustn't + verbProhibition; something is not allowedYou mustn't share your login details.
Don't + verbDirect instruction or warningDon't open unknown attachments.
Remember: Use the base form after these structures. Say must report, should check, mustn't share — NOT must to report, should checks or mustn't sharing.

Important difference: mustn't means “it is forbidden”. don't have to means “it isn't necessary”. They do not mean the same thing.

A · Choose the correct option

  1. You (should / shouldn't) make regular backups of important files.
  2. Employees (must / mustn't) share their passwords with colleagues.
  3. You (should / mustn't) report a lost company laptop immediately.
  4. (Don't / Doesn't) install software from unknown websites.
  5. Visitors (must / mustn't) enter the server room without permission.
  6. We (should / shouldn't) ignore security updates.
  7. You (must / should) follow your company's security policy. It is a strict rule.
  8. She (should / shoulds) contact the IT department.
  9. They must (change / to change) their temporary passwords.
  10. You shouldn't (click / clicking) suspicious links.

5. Case study: The security incident

10 min

Work as a cybersecurity consultant. Read the situation, identify the problems and recommend solutions.

Company: PixelPoint — a digital agency with 18 employees.

During one week, the IT manager discovers five problems:

  1. One employee uses the password pixel123 for six different accounts.
  2. A designer leaves a laptop unlocked when going to lunch.
  3. An employee downloads a free photo editor from an unknown website.
  4. A team member sends a document with customer details to a personal email address.
  5. The company hasn't made a backup for two months.

Your investigation

  1. Which problem is the most dangerous? Why?
  2. What could happen if the company ignores these problems?
  3. Write one rule for each problem. Use a different grammar structure where possible.
  4. Choose the three most important rules and explain your choices.
Possible recommendations1 The employee should create unique, strong passwords. · 2 Employees must lock their screens when they leave their desks. · 3 Don't download software from unknown websites. · 4 Employees mustn't send confidential information to personal email accounts. · 5 The company should make regular backups.

Questions to ask:

  • How often do you make backups?
  • Can employees install their own software?
  • Who has access to confidential information?
  • What do employees do when they notice suspicious activity?

Useful language:

  • You should… / You shouldn't…
  • Your employees must… / They mustn't…
  • Don't allow employees to…
  • The biggest risk is…
  • To prevent this problem, …

Challenge: use all five grammar structures correctly during the conversation.

7. Writing: Create a company security policy

Homework / 10 min

Imagine you work in the IT department at PixelPoint. Write a short security policy for all employees.

Your policy must include:

  • 2 rules with must
  • 2 prohibitions with mustn't
  • 2 recommendations with should / shouldn't
  • 2 direct warnings with Don't

Use at least five words: password, confidential, backup, access, encryption, update, employee, data breach, secure, permission.

Opening example: “At PixelPoint, protecting company information is everyone's responsibility. All employees must follow these security rules.”

Self-check:

  • Did I use all four grammar structures?
  • Did I use the base verb after each modal?
  • Are my rules clear and realistic?
  • Did I use at least five lesson vocabulary words?
